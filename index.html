Who We Are
A Togolese nonprofit born from the communities it serves

Founded on October 18, 1998 by young people from the Avé Prefecture who refused to accept the conditions they saw around them. Independent, non-political, and rooted in respect for local cultures.

Support Our Mission
Our Mission
Simple, urgent, and unwavering

To support underprivileged children by giving them access to education and essential healthcare. To walk alongside vulnerable communities — providing prevention, awareness, and access to basic health services. To stand with girls and young women so they can improve their living conditions, find their place in society, and move forward with dignity, strength and hope.

01

Education as a Right

Every child deserves school access. Our family sponsorship programs ensure children don't just enroll — they stay, graduate, and go further.

02

Health as a Foundation

Without health, nothing else is possible. We set up social and health infrastructure to relieve suffering and meet the needs of local communities.

03

Partnership, Not Dependency

Our work is rooted in win-win partnerships where everyone contributes and benefits. The community leads; we walk alongside.

04

Girls First

Through vocational training, rights promotion, and capacity building — we support girls' integration into working life with dignity.

05

Economic Autonomy

Income-generating activities — sponsorship, agriculture, vocational training — help families fund their children's schooling sustainably.

06

Active Citizenship

Civic education and community awareness build communities that advocate for themselves and hold space for one another.

Avé is divided into two municipalities across eight cantons. In each, live families, children, and young people facing multiple vulnerabilities. It is alongside them that we work every day.

Where APED is exceptional

Maternal Health (100%) and WASH Infrastructure (100%) demonstrate strong field execution capacity. Women's Economic Empowerment (92%) shows high community engagement and program-fit. These are areas where capital scales directly into outcomes.

Where targeted investment compounds

Vocational Training (61%) and Hygiene Kits Distribution (70%) underperformed primarily due to resource limitations. Each has a clear unit-cost path to scale — additional funding here removes the bottleneck rather than testing a new theory of change.

Insufficient resources for expanding vocational training initiatives.
Need for broader WASH awareness and behavior-change campaigns.
Limited access to primary healthcare infrastructure in rural communities.
Strategic Priority 2026 – 2028

Community Health Center — Kpotamé

Access to healthcare remains a major challenge in Kpotamé and surrounding rural communities. Many households travel long distances to access basic medical consultations, prenatal and postnatal care, child vaccinations, and emergency healthcare.

To address this critical gap, APED has identified the construction of a Community Health Center in Kpotamé as the strategic priority for 2026–2028.

  • Basic medical consultations
  • Prenatal and postnatal care
  • Child vaccination services
  • Emergency healthcare

Project Roadmap
From cleared ground to open doors

Four phases, built on transparency. Each quarter, donors receive photo and video updates — real progress, real stories.

Site Preparation & Foundation

The community of Kpotamé contributed their own land and labor. Site clearing is complete. This phase raises $180,000 to lay the foundation — the literal groundwork for everything that follows.
